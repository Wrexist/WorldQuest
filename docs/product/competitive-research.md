# Competitive research

Fourteen teardowns, each answering the five questions from the founder brief:
**What do they do well? What keeps users coming back? What do users complain about?
How do they monetise? Where can WorldQuest be meaningfully different?**

> reliable. **Pricing, user counts, and revenue figures change constantly — every

## Tier 1 — the ones we must beat

### Duolingo
**Does well** — the most complete habit machine in consumer education: streaks,
leagues, hearts, quests, a beloved mascot, and world-class notification craft. Lessons
are short and always available. Onboarding gets you to a completed lesson in under a
minute.
**Return driver** — loss aversion (streak + league demotion) and identity ("I'm a
person with a 400-day streak").
**Complaints** — gamification has drifted ahead of learning; users report high streaks
and low ability. Ad and paywall pressure on free tier. Notification tone reads as
guilt. Hearts blocking learning is the single most complained-about mechanic.
**Monetisation** — freemium subscription (Super/Max) + ads. `TODO(verify) pricing`.
**Our difference** — we take Duolingo's *retention* craft and reject its *pressure*.
Free topic choice instead of a forced path (Leo). Hearts never block learning. A
notification tone that invites. And we prove learning with a mastery model, not a
streak count.

### Seterra / GeoGuessr Learn
**Does well** — the reference geography drill tool: exhaustive coverage, instant map
interaction, no fluff, works in a browser, loved by teachers for 15 years.
**Return driver** — utility. Teachers assign it; students use it before a test.
**Complaints** — dated feel, thin mobile experience, no progression, no memory —
it tests you but never *schedules* you. No reason to return once the test is over.
**Monetisation** — freemium/ads under the GeoGuessr umbrella. `TODO(verify)`.
**Our difference** — Seterra is our content bar and our proof of demand. We add the
thing it structurally lacks: **spaced repetition and a reason to come back tomorrow.**

### GeoGuessr
**Does well** — the best *feeling* geography product ever made. Street View as a game
is genuinely magical, and it has a real competitive scene and creator ecosystem.
**Return driver** — social competition, streamers, daily challenge, ranked play.
**Complaints** — expensive, subscription-gated to a degree that annoys casual players,
and it assumes knowledge rather than teaching it. Steep for beginners.
**Monetisation** — subscription. `TODO(verify) pricing`.
**Our difference** — GeoGuessr is the *destination*; we are the *training ground*.
Explicit positioning: "learn the world here, then go play GeoGuessr and win." Not a
competitor — a potential funnel and eventual partner.

## Tier 2 — adjacent mechanics to learn from

### Kahoot
**Does well** — owns the classroom moment. Live, loud, social, zero student setup.
Teachers evangelise it.
**Return driver** — the teacher, not the student. Almost no organic student retention.
**Complaints** — chaotic, rewards fast fingers over knowledge, thin outside live play,
aggressive upsell to teachers who pay personally.
**Monetisation** — teacher/school subscriptions. `TODO(verify)`.
**Our difference** — Kahoot proves classroom distribution works and that *speed-only*
scoring is a learning flaw. Our classroom mode (v2.0) will be asynchronous and
mastery-scored — homework that works, not a game show.

### Brilliant
**Does well** — the best "learning feels intelligent" experience in the category.
Interactive, visual, respects adults, beautiful.
**Return driver** — intrinsic interest and a credible sense of getting smarter.
**Complaints** — expensive; hard content causes drop-off; weaker daily habit than
Duolingo; some courses feel thin.
**Monetisation** — premium subscription, mostly annual. `TODO(verify)`.
**Our difference** — Brilliant is our **quality and tone benchmark for adults**
(Priya, Ingrid, Kenji). We aim for Brilliant's dignity with Duolingo's habit.

### Quizlet
**Does well** — enormous user-generated content library and genuinely good spaced
repetition ("Learn" mode). Search-driven acquisition is unmatched.
**Return driver** — utility before exams; the content you need already exists.
**Complaints** — UGC quality is wildly uneven; aggressive paywalling of previously
free features caused real backlash; ads.
**Monetisation** — subscription + ads. `TODO(verify)`.
**Our difference** — Quizlet is the cautionary tale on **paywalling what used to be
free** and on **unmoderated UGC**. It's also proof that spaced repetition sells. Our
content is curated and sourced; UGC waits until v4.0 with moderation.

### Elevate · Peak · Lumosity — the brain-training cluster
**Do well** — polished daily-habit design, satisfying score progressions, strong
onboarding, good notification craft. Elevate in particular has excellent session pacing.
**Return driver** — daily workout framing, streaks, personalised score graphs.
**Complaints** — **the science is contested** (Lumosity settled with the US FTC in
2016 over deceptive advertising about cognitive benefits — this is well documented and
worth knowing). Repetitive minigames; hard paywall after a short trial; "it stops
being fun after a month".
**Monetisation** — subscription, hard paywall. `TODO(verify)`.
**Our difference** — the decisive one: **our learning claim is verifiable.** "You
know 183 of 195 countries" is checkable; "your brain is 12 % sharper" is not. We
inherit their session-pacing craft and none of their credibility problem.

## Tier 3 — the trivia/puzzle field

### Worldle (and the *-dle family)
**Does well** — perfect atomic design: one puzzle, one minute, one shareable result
grid. Zero friction, zero install.
**Return driver** — daily ritual + social sharing. The share grid is the entire growth
engine.
**Complaints** — thin; you finish and leave; no progression; clones everywhere.
**Monetisation** — barely; ads or acquisition.
**Our difference** — **steal the share grid.** A daily WorldQuest challenge with a
spoiler-free shareable result is our cheapest viral loop, and it costs a week of work.
High priority for v1.5.

### Sporcle
**Does well** — huge quiz library, strong community, "name all X in Y minutes" is
addictive, great SEO.
**Return driver** — community, new quizzes daily, competitive completion.
**Complaints** — heavy ads, dated UI, weak mobile, no learning structure at all.
**Monetisation** — ads + ad-free subscription. `TODO(verify)`.
**Our difference** — Sporcle *tests*; it never *teaches*. We convert its "name all
195" thrill into a Speed Round mode, backed by an engine that fixes what you missed.

### JetPunk
**Does well** — clean, fast, huge geography catalogue, excellent web experience.
**Return driver** — daily quizzes, personal bests.
**Complaints** — web-only feel, no mobile app worth using, no progression, no memory.
**Monetisation** — ads. `TODO(verify)`.
**Our difference** — the same gap as Sporcle. JetPunk is a strong signal that
geography quiz demand is large, evergreen, and badly served on mobile.

### StudySmarter
**Does well** — study-planning, spaced repetition, and community notes aimed at
university students.
**Return driver** — exam deadlines and study plans.
**Complaints** — cluttered, feature-sprawled, unfocused; content quality varies.
**Monetisation** — freemium subscription. `TODO(verify)`.
**Our difference** — focus. StudySmarter shows what happens when a learning product
tries to be everything: it stops being anyone's favourite.

### Atlas Mission
**Does well** — early-years (5–10) adaptive learning with a game wrapper; parent
reporting done properly.
**Return driver** — parents, mostly.
**Complaints** — young-skewing production values; limited depth; ageing.
**Monetisation** — parent subscription. `TODO(verify)`. *(Verify current status —
this product may no longer be actively developed.)*
**Our difference** — it validates the **parent dashboard** (Marcus) but shows the
trap of building *only* for kids: you age out your users. WorldQuest deliberately
spans 10 → 67 with one product and a Relaxed Mode.

## Synthesis — the five gaps we are aiming at

| # | Gap | Who leaves it open | Our answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Geography with real memory science** | Seterra, Sporcle, JetPunk, GeoGuessr test but never schedule; Duolingo and Quizlet schedule but not geography | FSRS per-fact mastery over a geography content pack |
| **2** | **Habit without pressure** | Duolingo's mechanics are excellent and increasingly disliked | Same loop, kind constraints: hearts never block learning, no shame copy, Relaxed Mode |
| **3** | **A verifiable learning claim** | Brain-training's claims are contested; Duolingo's streak ≠ ability | "183 / 195 countries, 85 % retained at 30 days" — checkable |
| **4** | **One product from 10 to 67** | Kids' apps age out; adult apps bore kids | One engine, two presentation modes, a Family plan that covers both |
| **5** | **A subject-agnostic engine** | Everyone in this list is locked to their vertical | Content-as-data → history, wildlife, space are packs |

## What we must steal, deliberately

1. **Duolingo's onboarding** — a completed lesson before an account is required.
2. **Worldle's share grid** — spoiler-free, text-only, instantly recognisable.
3. **Brilliant's respect for adults** — visual, interactive, never condescending.
4. **Seterra's content completeness** — the full world, not the popular 60.
5. **Elevate's session pacing** — the app ends the session before you do.
6. **Kahoot's zero-setup class code** — no student emails, ever.

## What we must not copy

Duolingo's guilt notifications · Quizlet's re-paywalling of free features ·
Lumosity's unfalsifiable claims · Sporcle's ad density · Kahoot's speed-over-knowledge
scoring · anyone's loot boxes.
